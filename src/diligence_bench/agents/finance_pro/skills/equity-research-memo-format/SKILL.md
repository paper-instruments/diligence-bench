# equity-research-memo-format

## When to invoke

Use this for diligence questions that need a written investment view rather than a single fact lookup.

## Format

A good memo is usually 600-1200 words. It should answer first, then support the answer with evidence and reasoning.

### 1. Thesis

Open with the answer in one or two sentences. Commit to a directional view.

Good:

"The change-of-control event-of-default in the SVB facility is the fastest and most severe liquidity risk; the $40M undrawn capacity is not dependable liquidity unless a waiver or amendment exists."

Weak:

"There are several factors that could affect liquidity, and additional analysis may be warranted."

### 2. Key Facts

List 5-10 facts with exact figures, dates, units, and source labels.

Example:

- **Reported AQR**: 1.13 as of March 31, 2020 (Q1 2020 10-Q, Item 2 MD&A).
- **Covenant threshold**: 1.00 to 1.05 range, not 1.00 alone (Q1 2020 10-Q, Note 7 Debt).
- **AQR cushion**: 1.13 - 1.05 = 8 bps to the upper threshold; 1.13 - 1.00 = 13 bps to the lower threshold.
- **Facility maturity**: September 26, 2020 (Q1 2020 10-Q, Note 7).
- **Undrawn balance**: $40.0M as of March 31, 2020, maturing on the same date.

Keep facts separate from inference. A reader should be able to audit every number.

### 3. Mechanism

Explain why the facts matter. Name the causal chain.

Useful patterns:

- "X compresses liquidity because..."
- "Y raises dilution risk because..."
- "Z is the primary driver rather than a side effect because..."
- "The exposure is binary, not gradual, because..."
- "The risk is timing-driven, not solvency-driven, because..."

Avoid restating the key facts in softer language. The mechanism section should explain how the facts move the investment view.

### 4. Risk Triage

Rank the risks. Use explicit dimensions:

- Severity
- Timing
- Reversibility
- Management control
- Disclosure dependence
- Market sensitivity

Example:

1. **Change-of-control default**: highest severity and fastest timing; it can crystallize immediately on merger close.
2. **AQR covenant pressure**: medium timing; it depends on quarter-end testing and operating cash flow stress.
3. **Facility maturity**: slower timing; it has a visible runway if no other default blocks refinancing.

### 5. Counter-Evidence

State the strongest evidence against your thesis. If there is no material counter-evidence in the filings, say that plainly.

Do not invent false balance. A thin counter-evidence section is better than a manufactured one.

### 6. Assumptions

Name 2-5 assumptions that the view depends on.

Example:

- Assumes the company had not obtained a waiver for the change-of-control provision.
- Assumes the undrawn revolver was subject to the same borrowing conditions at the balance-sheet date.
- Assumes reported liquidity does not include restricted cash unless explicitly disclosed.

### 7. Missing Disclosures

Name the information that would most change the conclusion.

Example:

- No disclosed waiver or amendment for the change-of-control provision.
- No disclosure of whether the borrower was in a streamline or non-streamline period.
- No quantification of incremental infrastructure costs from elevated request volume.
- No refinancing plan for the September 2020 maturity.

Rank missing disclosures by importance when possible.

### 8. Conclusion

End with one direct sentence. Do not end with "more research is needed."

Example:

"Net: the credit view is materially negative unless the company can show a valid waiver and continued access to the undrawn facility."

## Density Check

Before submitting, make sure the memo contains:

- Exact figures with dates and units.
- Primary-source citations where available.
- Visible arithmetic for derived metrics.
- At least one causal mechanism.
- At least one ranked risk or structural distinction.
- Named assumptions.
- Named missing disclosures.
- A committed directional conclusion.

## Avoid

- Restating the question as the introduction.
- Long background history before the answer.
- Unsupported adjectives.
- Rounded figures when exact figures are available.
- "Could be", "may be", and "potentially" as substitutes for judgment.
- Ending with generic monitoring language.
