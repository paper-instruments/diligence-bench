# sec-filing-citation-discipline

## When to invoke

Use this any time an answer relies on a figure, date, ratio, covenant term,
segment definition, accounting policy, debt term, compensation item, merger
term, customer concentration, or other disclosure sourced from an SEC filing.

## Citation anatomy

A useful filing citation identifies:

1. Filer.
2. Filing type: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, S-1, S-4, 13F, 13D, etc.
3. Period or filing date.
4. Section, item, note, exhibit, or table.
5. Exact claim supported by that source.

Complete example:

"Per the FY 2024 10-K (Item 8, Note 17 Segment Information), Specialty P&C
international net written premiums were $42.8M, sourced entirely from one
strategic partnership."

Weak examples:

- "The 10-K shows international NWP of $42.8M." Missing period and section.
- "Per recent filings, international premiums are around $42.8M." "Recent
  filings" is not a source, and "around" weakens an exact figure.
- "International NWP is $42.8M (2025)." Missing filing type and location.

## Filing quick reference

| Filing | Covers | Useful sections |
|---|---|---|
| 10-K | Full fiscal year | Item 1 Business, Item 1A Risk Factors, Item 7 MD&A, Item 8 Financial Statements and Notes |
| 10-Q | Fiscal quarter | Item 1 Financial Statements and Notes, Item 2 MD&A, Item 3 Market Risk, Item 4 Controls |
| 8-K | Material current events | Item 1.01 Material Agreements, Item 2.02 Results, Item 5.02 Officers/Directors, Exhibit 99.1 |
| DEF 14A | Proxy statement | Compensation tables, ownership, governance, shareholder proposals |
| S-1 / S-4 | IPO or transaction registration | Prospectus Summary, Risk Factors, MD&A, Use of Proceeds, transaction terms |
| 13F | Institutional holdings | Holdings table with securities, shares, value |
| 13D / 13G | Beneficial ownership | Ownership, purpose of transaction, amendments |

## Period discipline

- A 10-K filed in 2025 usually covers fiscal year 2024. Say "FY 2024 10-K
  filed 2025-02-28" when the filing date matters.
- A 10-Q covers the quarter ended on its balance-sheet date, not necessarily a
  calendar quarter.
- For 8-Ks, use the filing date or event date, depending on what the question
  asks.
- Proxy compensation figures usually reflect the prior fiscal year even when
  the proxy is filed in the meeting year.

Do not call a fiscal-year filing by the calendar year of its filing if that
would confuse the period being analyzed.

## Restatements and re-segmentation

Use the most recent filing that restates the period being compared. If a 2024
10-K restates 2022 revenue, cite the 2024 10-K for the restated 2022 figure.

For segment questions:

- Read the most recent segment footnote before comparing periods.
- Confirm the segment definition matches the question.
- Use restated historical figures when a company changes segment definitions.

## Covenant and contract terms

For credit agreements, indentures, merger agreements, leases, or supply
contracts, stay close to the operative language. Small wording differences can
change the conclusion.

Useful format:

"The facility sets a minimum Adjusted Quick Ratio of 1.00x, increasing to
1.05x upon a specified streamline event (Q1 2020 10-Q, Note 7)."

If the exhibit contains the controlling term and the filing summary only
paraphrases it, cite the exhibit when possible.

## Non-GAAP and company-defined metrics

Adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, organic growth, same-community revenue,
adjusted EPS, and similar metrics are company-defined. Use the company's
reconciliation table and definition.

Do not rebuild a non-GAAP metric from raw line items unless the answer clearly
labels it as your own derived estimate and explains why the company-disclosed
figure is unavailable or unsuitable.

## Defaults when unsure

- Prefer a specific but imperfect citation over a vague one.
- If the section was not captured, say so rather than inventing it.
- If a figure cannot be confirmed, state the gap and do not blend sources
  opaquely.
- If two sources disagree, use the primary filing or the later restatement, and
  explain the conflict.
